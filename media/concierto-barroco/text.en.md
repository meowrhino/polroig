> “The true image of the past speeds away. The past can only be held as a shining image that threatens to vanish with every present that does not recognise itself in it.” Walter Benjamin

*Concierto barroco* is a musical and scenic exploration of the novel of the same title, written by Alejo Carpentier in 1974. In a dialogue-free staging, a series of cultural objects serve as a narrative thread, alongside the performance of different musical works drawn from the novel. The musicians themselves, portrayed as the composers and characters who appear in the novel, become the protagonists of an exercise that seeks to reconnect the musical works with their original contexts, dismantling the historical sources behind the fictional device that allows Carpentier to imagine the gaps in the official narrative.

Two panels bearing projected text hang above the stage and function as a supplement, re-signifying and exhaustively cataloguing—with Carpentier’s characteristic irony—everything displayed in this imaginary museum and brought within the viewer’s sight and hearing.

Commentary
*by Pol Roig and David Aguilar*

*Wunderkammern*, cabinets of curiosities, were rooms (or sometimes simple pieces of furniture) where the nobility and bourgeoisie of the 16th, 17th and 18th centuries collected and displayed objects of very diverse origins, from works of art to scientific curiosities and exotic objects. Direct ancestors of modern museums, these galleries were characterised by the saturation of space and the eclecticism of the treasures accumulated in them. The *wunderkammer* is thus a privileged example of the Baroque aesthetic which, as Borges says, plays at exhausting its own possibilities, and it is the basis of the stage device of this piece.

> “Vivaldi’s work is a perpetual feast, and I wanted to make a kind of verbal feast.” Alejo Carpentier

Unlike the *white box* characteristic of the modern museum, the mechanism of the cabinet of curiosities is able to reveal unexpected links, continuities and contrasts between objects, times, spaces and stories. With its abyssal structures, the hypertrophy of its forms and its declared artificiality, the dialogue with the Baroque presents itself as an alternative to the homogenisation and standardisation of present-day categories, guided by the logic of consumption and by an idea of single progress that tends to erase diversity in favour of a clear, refined and singular reading.

[[galeria:bocetos | Preliminary sketches by Pol Roig, in ink on tracing paper.]]

In this way, the *Concierto barroco* presented here draws on the eclecticism and excess of the *wunderkammer* to explore the sounds, images and narratives that come from the past—or rather, from which we construct the past, and even more so, the past of others. Because, ultimately, understanding what precedes us means learning to interpret it, that is, to elaborate it, to trace possible readings among all the signs (visual, verbal and sonic) that besiege and saturate us. In this sense, to baroque-ify can be a playful way of claiming the complexity and density of this process, combining original and copy, essence and supplement, history and fiction, in order to detect the grey areas where everything mixes and turns upside down.

> “Allegory is a supplement. Conceived as something added to the work, allegory will be separable from it.” Craig Owens
